Understanding the Mesozoic

The three great periods.

Triassic, Jurassic, Cretaceous: three chapters of the Mesozoic to place dinosaurs in time.

01Triassic252–201 Ma02Jurassic201–145 Ma03Cretaceous145–66 Ma

Ma = million years. Rounded landmarks based on the cited articles; dates may be revised. Schematic timeline, not to scale.

Before the dinosaurs

Permian

The Permian precedes the Triassic and belongs to the Palaeozoic. Dimetrodon lived during this period: this synapsid is more closely related to mammals than to dinosaurs. The Permian ended with a mass extinction about 252 million years ago.

Harvard Museum of Natural History ↗ · Natural History Museum ↗

01 · About 252–201 million years ago

Triassic

The first dinosaurs

Most land forms Pangaea, a vast supercontinent surrounded by the Panthalassa ocean. The climate is generally warm, with extensive dry regions.

Life diversifies again after the end-Permian crisis. The first dinosaurs, pterosaurs and mammals appear during the Triassic. Another major extinction marks its end.

Source: French Wikipedia - Trias ↗

02 · About 201–145 million years ago

Jurassic

Dinosaurs diversify

Pangaea breaks apart. New oceans reshape the world, while shallow seas cover parts of Europe.

Dinosaurs are prominent on land. Giant sauropods live alongside predatory theropods, and the first birds appear. The Jurassic lies between the Triassic and the Cretaceous.

Source: French Wikipedia - Jurassique ↗

03 · About 145–66 million years ago

Cretaceous

A changing world

Continents continue to separate and sea levels are high. Flowering plants spread and diversify.

This is the final period of the Mesozoic. It ends with a mass extinction associated notably with the Chicxulub impact: non-avian dinosaurs disappear, but birds survive.

Source: French Wikipedia - Crétacé ↗
